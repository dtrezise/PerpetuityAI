02 The problem

The asset moves faster
than the authority.

Digital likeness, voice, and performance assets can cross vendors, productions, and media long after their original capture. The documentation that governs them rarely travels as well.

A / 01
⌁

Permission fragments

Consent lives across contracts, email, call sheets, deal memos, and disconnected systems.

Result: unclear authority
A / 02
◎

Assets keep traveling

Scans, recordings, rigs, and derived models persist across tools and counterparties.

Result: invisible exposure
A / 03
↻

Rules keep changing

Union terms, state laws, project conditions, and performer approvals evolve over time.

Result: expensive rework

03 The working thesis

The next durable layer is not another model.
It is accountable permission.

Perpetuity AI is conceived as neutral infrastructure above capture and generation tools. It does not decide whether a replica should exist. It records who may decide, under which terms, and with what evidence.

POSITIONING Rights orchestration Not generation

06 Trust principles

Infrastructure worthy
of an identity.

A digital replica cannot be reset like a password. The product has to minimize custody, narrow access, and make every consequential action visible.

01

Specific by default

No perpetual blanket permission. Every request has a defined purpose and boundary.

02

Minimal custody

Keep raw biometric payloads with trusted custodians whenever the workflow permits.

03

Future-use control

Expiration and revocation govern what may happen next without rewriting the past.

04

Vendor neutral

Rights travel across approved capture, generation, storage, and production systems.

05

Human approval

Algorithms may provide evidence; they do not unilaterally determine consent or pay.

06

Inspectable history

Every authorization, change, access event, and usage record remains attributable.

07 Launch thesis

Start with one
complete chain.

The first proof is not a marketplace. It is one real, paid, authorized transaction that moves from request to consent, production use, and compensation without losing its evidence.

  1. NOW
    Discover

    Choose one production buyer and one bounded digital-replica use case.

  2. 01
    Model

    Define the rights graph, consent lifecycle, threat model, and pilot terms.

  3. 02
    Pilot

    Run the workflow with paid design partners before building a broad platform.

  4. 03
    Harden

    Turn validated operations into secure, repeatable, enterprise-grade software.

  5. LATER
    Expand

    Add vendors and markets only after repeat demand proves transaction density.
