Private pilot

01 Trust posture

Protect the person.
Minimize the blast radius.

This page states the intended security and privacy posture for a pre-production private pilot. It distinguishes design commitments from controls that still require implementation and independent assessment.

Current statusNo production platform, formal certification, or independent security assessment is claimed.

02 System boundary

A control plane, not an asset plane.

AUTHORITY PLANEPeople, representatives, contracts, counsel
↓
CONTROL PLANERequests, decisions, instructions, exceptions
ASSET PLANEProtected assets remain with approved custodians
EVIDENCE PLANEVersioned records and reviewable exports

The pilot should store only what is necessary to operate and evidence the workflow. Any proposal to centralize raw biometric, performance, or model files requires a separate necessity test, threat model, legal review, retention rule, and buyer approval.

03 Design commitments

The minimum credible posture.

01

Data minimization

Collect the smallest data set required for a named purpose, project, and retention period.

02

Human authorization

Automated systems may organize evidence; they do not independently grant consent or determine legal rights.

03

Role and project isolation

Access should be limited to approved roles, projects, fields, and time windows with attributable changes.

04

Secure handoff

Instructions and asset references should be transmitted through approved channels with acknowledgement and exception handling.

05

Retention and disposition

Every data class needs an owner, retention basis, disposition event, and exception path.

06

Incident readiness

Pilots require named escalation contacts, containment steps, evidence preservation, and notification decision paths.

04 Priority threats

Design against foreseeable misuse.

ThreatInitial response
Unauthorized requester or representativeIndependent authority checks, conflict flags, separation of duties, and documented reviewer decision.
Use outside approved scopeExplicit boundaries, versioned instructions, acknowledgements, exception routing, and closeout confirmation.
Sensitive-data overcollectionField-level necessity review, references instead of payloads, retention limits, and deletion verification.
Record tampering or ambiguityAppend-only event history, version identifiers, timestamps, attributable actions, and signed exports where justified.
Cross-project or vendor leakageTenant and project isolation, least privilege, secure delivery, vendor diligence, and revocation of access.
False confidence in provenanceSeparate technical integrity from legal truth and preserve the issuer and basis of every authority assertion.

05 Assurance roadmap

Controls before claims.

  1. BEFORE LIVE DATA

    Data inventory, threat model, access matrix, approved tools, incident path, retention schedule, vendor review, participant notice, and counsel-reviewed pilot terms.

  2. BEFORE REPEAT PILOTS

    Centralized identity, multifactor authentication, environment separation, structured audit events, backup and restoration tests, secure-development checks, and documented deletion tests.

  3. BEFORE ENTERPRISE CLAIMS

    Independent penetration testing, formal security and privacy program, control evidence, contractual subprocessors, response exercises, and an appropriate external assurance path.

06 Responsible disclosure

Report a security concern.

Please do not include biometric files, credentials, confidential contracts, or personal data in an initial report. Describe the affected surface, reproduction steps, and potential impact.
