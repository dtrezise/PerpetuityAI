# Private-pilot playbook

Status: proposed  
Duration: 12 weeks  
Commercial posture: paid discovery and concierge operation  
Last updated: 2026-10-07

## Objective

Determine whether a production-side buyer will pay for a repeatable authorization workflow that improves speed, completeness, and accountability without requiring Perpetuity AI to centralize raw biometric assets.

## Entry gate

A pilot may start only when:

- an accountable sponsor owns the current process and budget;
- a real, bounded digital-replica transaction is available;
- required stakeholders can participate;
- counsel or qualified reviewers can define the decision boundary;
- approved tools and data categories are documented;
- baseline measures and stop conditions are agreed in writing; and
- separate pilot terms cover confidentiality, data handling, security, fees, work product, liability, and closeout.

## Phase 1 — Baseline, weeks 1–2

Interview the sponsor, business affairs, production, rights-holder representative, and vendor participants. Observe the current process. Inventory systems and documents. Measure:

- elapsed time from request to decision;
- active reviewer time;
- clarification loops;
- missing or conflicting fields;
- duplicate entry;
- material exceptions;
- rework after vendor handoff;
- evidence available at closeout; and
- stakeholder confidence using a consistent scale.

Deliverable: current-state map and pilot risk register.

## Phase 2 — Define, weeks 3–4

Agree on:

- exact in-scope transaction;
- authorization-packet fields and evidence;
- authority and conflict rules;
- human approval and escalation points;
- role-specific views and vendor instructions;
- data inventory, custodian, retention, and disposition;
- outcome measures and target ranges; and
- pause and stop conditions.

Deliverable: signed-off pilot protocol and packet template.

## Phase 3 — Operate, weeks 5–9

Run the live workflow. Maintain:

- versioned request;
- evidence index;
- decision and exception log;
- vendor delivery and acknowledgement record;
- incident and deviation log; and
- weekly measure snapshot.

Do not silently repair missing evidence. Record the failure, responsible owner, correction, and impact.

Deliverable: operated transaction and weekly evidence log.

## Phase 4 — Close out, weeks 10–12

Confirm final use, delivery, retention or disposition, exceptions, approvals, and compensation-event references. Compare outcomes with baseline. Conduct structured participant interviews.

Deliverable: redacted authorization packet, outcome report, control gaps, product backlog, and recommendation.

## Decision gate

### Build

Proceed when the buyer pays, the workflow repeats, measurable friction decreases, reviewers find the packet useful, risk remains within the agreed boundary, and the sponsor wants another transaction without founder-dependent effort.

### Revise

Repeat a narrowed pilot when value appears credible but the user, transaction, integration, or packet is wrong.

### Stop

Stop or reposition when buyers will not pay, incumbent systems solve the problem adequately, the workflow requires disproportionate raw-asset custody, authority cannot be represented reliably, or support cost prevents a credible business.

## Interview discipline

- Ask for recent concrete examples, not opinions about the future.
- Separate the user, beneficiary, approver, budget owner, and risk owner.
- Capture direct quotes only with permission and context.
- Do not treat enthusiasm, introductions, or unpaid participation as purchase evidence.
- Store raw notes in an approved private location; publish only redacted synthesis.

## Evidence package

The final package should contain a source index, assumptions, unresolved disagreements, baseline, outcome, exceptions, control evidence, participant feedback, and the sponsor’s documented decision.
