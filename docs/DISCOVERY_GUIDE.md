# Customer-discovery guide

Status: interview standard  
Last updated: 2026-10-07

## Research question

Which production-side digital-replica transaction is frequent, consequential, and standardized enough that an accountable buyer will pay to improve its authorization and handoff process?

## Sampling plan

Interview at least 12 people across distinct roles:

- production or studio business affairs;
- legal operations or clearance;
- production technology, VFX, or innovation;
- producer or production executive;
- performer, rights holder, agent, or estate representative;
- approved capture, AI, post-production, or VFX vendor; and
- security, privacy, or records owner.

Do not treat several interviews from the same organization or role as independent market validation.

## Opening

Explain that the conversation concerns the participant’s current process, not a product sale. Request permission before recording or attributing quotations. Do not ask for confidential documents, names, or sensitive asset details.

## Core questions

1. Tell me about the most recent digital-likeness, voice, or performance use you personally handled.
2. What triggered the request, and who was accountable for the decision?
3. Which people, teams, vendors, and systems were involved?
4. What information did each reviewer need?
5. Where did contracts, approvals, asset records, and instructions live?
6. Which information was missing, inconsistent, or late?
7. What changed between the original capture or approval and the later use?
8. How did a vendor learn what was permitted and prohibited?
9. What exceptions or escalations occurred?
10. How was final use, retention, deletion, or compensation confirmed?
11. How much elapsed time and active work did the process require?
12. What error or delay would be materially costly?
13. What existing system owns this process today?
14. Why is the current system insufficient, if it is?
15. Who owns the budget for improvement?
16. What would block adoption of a new workflow?
17. Which data must never enter a new system?
18. What evidence would counsel, a rights holder, or an auditor expect later?
19. Would you run this process again in the next 12 months?
20. What would have to be true to pay for a pilot?

## Evidence capture

For each interview, record:

- role and organization type;
- date and consent to quote;
- recent transaction described;
- buyer, user, approver, beneficiary, and risk owner;
- current tools and source documents;
- frequency and severity;
- time, rework, error, and support evidence;
- current alternative;
- willingness to pay and procurement constraints;
- contradictory evidence; and
- claims updated in the register.

## Interpretation rules

- A future opinion is weaker than a recent example.
- Pain without budget is not a buyer.
- Unpaid design participation is not purchase evidence.
- An introduction is not demand.
- A request for a feature is not proof of a repeatable workflow.
- Preserve negative evidence and reasons to use an incumbent.
